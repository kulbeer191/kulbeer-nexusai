Coda vs Notion AI (2026)

Both are the doc-workspace your team actually lives in, but they optimise for different things. Coda (now Superhuman Docs) is a doc that behaves like an app — relational tables, buttons, formulas and automations turn a page into a tracker, dashboard or workflow, and only Doc Makers pay. Notion AI is the all-in-one workspace — wikis, docs and project tracking with AI writing, search and meeting notes built in, billed per seat. Pick Coda to build your operating system; pick Notion AI for the workspace everyone already knows.

Coda — Pros

  • Docs that work like apps — relational tables, buttons, formulas, automations and 600+ Packs (integrations) turn a page into a tracker, dashboard or workflow; no other doc tool in the directory goes this far
  • Maker-only billing — only Doc Makers (people who create or structure docs) pay; editors and viewers are free on every plan, so a 20-person team with 3 builders pays for 3, not 20
  • Real free plan — $0 with collaborative docs, Docs AI trial, MCP trial, connected tables/views/forms, formulas and automations
  • Now bundled with the Superhuman suite — annual Pro includes Grammarly, Business adds Superhuman Go and Mail, so teams already buying those get them in the same bill; Docs AI (Beta) builds trackers and pages and answers questions across the workspace

Coda — Cons

  • The rename is genuinely confusing — coda.io/pricing redirects to superhuman.com/plans/docs, the page is titled 'Superhuman Docs', and the suite bundles Grammarly/Go/Mail in ways that make it hard to tell what you are actually buying
  • List prices rose at the rename — old widely-cited figures were Pro $10 / Team $30 per Doc Maker; the current published figures are $12 / $33 billed annually. The vendor's launch post says existing customers' pricing 'isn't changing', but new buyers pay the higher list prices — confirm at checkout
  • Docs AI is still in Beta — limited trial on Free, and the AI views/pages features are Beta; the AI story is newer and less proven than Notion AI's
  • 'Doc Maker' is a fuzzy billing unit — anyone who creates or structures docs counts, so a team that thought it had 3 makers can discover it has 8; and Grammarly/Superhuman ownership raised data questions in the Dec 2024 acquisition thread

Notion AI — Pros

  • Lives inside the workspace your team already uses
  • Full AI suite on Business: writing, search, meeting notes, agent
  • Strong for wikis, docs and project tracking
  • Enterprise search across connected apps

Notion AI — Cons

  • Full AI requires the Business plan — no standalone add-on for new users
  • Business price rose from $15 to $20/user/mo (annual) in 2025 — sources still disagree, confirm at checkout
  • AI runs under credit limits that can pause access
  • Less powerful as a general chatbot than ChatGPT or Claude

Frequently asked questions

Which is cheaper, Coda or Notion AI?

It depends on how many people build. Coda bills only Doc Makers — a 20-person team with 3 builders pays for 3 ($36/mo on Pro annual). Notion AI bills per seat — full AI needs Business at $20/user/mo annual, so the same 20-person team pays $400/mo. If everyone builds, Notion AI's per-seat price can win; if only a few build, Coda's maker billing wins by a wide margin.

Is Coda the same as Superhuman?

No — and this is the confusing part. Coda became Superhuman Docs on July 8, 2026, after Grammarly (which acquired Coda in Dec 2024) renamed the parent company to Superhuman. The doc product is now Superhuman Docs, but it is a different product from Superhuman the email client, which is also listed in this directory. The product is the same as Coda — all docs, workflows, tables, Packs, automations and formulas carry over.

Which is better for a team that already lives in Notion?

Notion AI. If your wikis, docs and project tracking already live in Notion, its AI is the path of least resistance — writing, search and meeting notes inside the workspace your team already uses. Coda is the deeper tool for teams that want docs that run like software, but switching costs are real.

Verdict

Pick Coda if you want docs that behave like apps — relational tables, buttons, formulas and automations that turn a page into a tracker or a whole operating system — and you want maker-only billing where only the builders pay. Pick Notion AI if your team already lives in Notion and wants AI writing, search and meeting notes inside the workspace it knows. If only a few people build and everyone else reads, Coda's maker billing wins on price by a wide margin.
