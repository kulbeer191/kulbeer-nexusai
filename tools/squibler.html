Squibler Review 2026

A browser-based AI writing studio that turns a chat prompt into a full-length manuscript — novel, nonfiction book or screenplay — with AI editing, scene expansion, character/plot tracking, built-in book-cover image generation and PDF/DOCX export.

Overview

US authors, novelists and screenwriters face a blank page and months of drafting — outlining a 200-300-page book, keeping characters and plot consistent, and revising without losing the thread is slow, lonely work. Squibler is a browser-based AI writing studio that turns a chat prompt into a full-length manuscript (novel, nonfiction book or screenplay), with AI editing, scene expansion, character/plot tracking, built-in book-cover image generation and PDF/DOCX export — plus a free physical copy of the first book you create with it.

Official site: https://www.squibler.io/

Key Features

The capabilities that matter most for US teams:

  • Full-length 200-300-page manuscripts generated from a prompt
  • AI Smart Writer — expand scenes, refine plot arcs, rework lines
  • Character, plot and structure tracking across the whole draft
  • AI book-cover/illustration generation built in; PDF, DOCX and EPUB export
  • 80+ language translation, word-count goals, collaboration

✅ Pros

  • Only tool in the directory built for whole books — generates full-length 200-300-page manuscripts from a prompt, not just chapters or short copy, with character, plot and structure kept consistent across the draft
  • Real free plan with no time limit — the free 'Limited Tier' never expires and your work stays accessible in free mode; you can test full book and screenplay creation before paying
  • Built for the whole author workflow — AI Smart Writer (expand scenes, refine plot arcs, rework lines), guided introduction tool, word-count goals, story-element generation, collaboration, 80+ language translation, and AI book-cover/illustration generation built in
  • You own everything — 100% author rights to all work created; exports to PDF and DOCX (Plus/Pro); EPUB export added November 13, 2026; 20,000+ writers use it

❌ Cons

  • Credit-metered writing — every AI action burns credits, and the free tier's allowance is small, so a serious book quickly needs a paid plan; Pro at ~$49/mo is steep for a hobbyist author
  • Review quality is uneven — independent 2026 reviews note fast first drafts but slow, sometimes shallow rewrites; the AI can drift on long manuscripts and still needs a human editor
  • No publishing or sales tools — no built-in publishing, sales tracking or audiobook production (it only prepares clean, well-paced text for export to self-publishing platforms and voice tools)
  • Annual discount is steep enough to distort the choice — Plus is $29.99/mo monthly but $15.83/mo billed annually (~$190/yr upfront), so the real decision is the yearly commitment, not the monthly figure

Frequently asked questions

Is Squibler free?

Yes. The free 'Limited Tier' costs $0 with no time limit — you can test full book and screenplay creation before paying, and your work stays accessible in free mode. The exact free credit allowance is disputed across sources (500 vs 1,000 a month), so confirm it on the official site. Paid plans start at Plus, $29.99 a month ($15.83 billed annually).

What is Squibler best for?

US authors and screenwriters who want a whole book drafted from a prompt — an indie author drafting a full novel or nonfiction book from an outline in weeks instead of months, a screenwriter developing a screenplay with AI scene expansion and character tracking, or a business author turning expertise into a lead-magnet book.

What should I watch out for with Squibler?

Credit-metered writing — every AI action burns credits, and the free tier's allowance is small and disputed across sources. Pro at ~$49 a month is steep for a hobbyist author, and the AI can drift on long manuscripts and still needs a human editor.

Verdict

Best for: US authors and screenwriters who want a whole book drafted from a prompt with a genuinely free starting tier — with the warning that the credit meter (the free allowance is small and sources disagree on its exact size) and the steep annual commitment behind the headline monthly price.
