Overview

Coding agents live in editors or CLIs, but the terminal is where builds, tests, deploys and SSH sessions actually happen — and every agent bills its own opaque way. Mel is an agentic terminal: a team of agents that plans, builds, tests and ships work from the terminal (with a VS Code extension too), on any model, with transparent credit math — and the whole terminal plus the agent are free if you bring your own provider key.

Official site: https://www.openmel.dev/

Key Features

The capabilities that matter most for US teams:

  • Full terminal free forever — blocks, palette, splits, chips, code review, editor with real LSP
  • AI agent on your own provider key at your provider's price, 0 Mel credits
  • Multi-agent orchestration — up to 3 parallel agents on Pro, 8 on Max
  • Transparent credit math — 1 credit = 1 US cent; turns billed at model cost + 20%
  • CLI agents (Claude Code, Codex) embedded at 0 Mel credits; SSH melting

✅ Pros

  • The full terminal is free forever — blocks, palette, splits, chips, built-in code review, editor with real LSP (rust-analyzer, tsserver, pyright, clangd), no account needed to download
  • Genuinely free AI on your own key — the agent runs on your provider key at your provider's price, 0 Mel credits, no markup; Groq and Gemini both have free key tiers, so the agent can cost $0
  • Multi-agent orchestration — a team of agents plans, builds, tests and ships; up to 3 parallel agents on Pro, 8 on Max; CLI agents (Claude Code, Codex) embedded at 0 Mel credits; SSH melting runs the agent on a remote host
  • Transparent credit math — 1 credit = 1 US cent; a turn costs what the model actually cost plus 20%; no per-token surprise billing; credits reset monthly and add-on packs roll over

❌ Cons

  • It is a terminal, not an editor — teams that want an AI-first IDE (Cursor-style) will miss the editor-centric workflow, though a VS Code extension exists
  • The free tier needs your own API key to use the agent at all — no hosted credits without paying
  • The Business plan is US-model-providers only (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, xAI, Groq) — DeepSeek, Qwen, Kimi and GLM are not available on team plans
  • Very young product — a new entrant from Thinkverse AI LLC with a small track record; long-term maintenance is unproven versus Cursor or Copilot

Frequently asked questions

Is Mel free?

Yes. The Free plan costs $0 forever with the full terminal and the AI agent on your own provider key at your provider's price — Groq and Gemini both have free key tiers, so the agent can cost $0. No card is required. Paid plans start at Pro, $20 a month ($18 billed annually), for hosted credits without managing keys.

What is Mel best for?

US developers who live in the terminal and want a team of agents on any model at cost — a backend team running a multi-agent crew from the terminal (one agent writes the feature, another runs the tests, a third fixes CI), a solo dev on a budget running the full agent on a free Groq or Gemini key, or a small team sharing one Business credit pool on one invoice.

What should I watch out for with Mel?

It is a terminal, not an editor — teams that want an AI-first IDE will miss the editor-centric workflow. The free tier needs your own API key to use the agent at all. And it is a very young product from Thinkverse AI LLC with a small track record.

Verdict

Best for: US developers who live in the terminal and want a team of agents on any model at cost — with the warning that it is a terminal, not an editor, and the free agent needs your own API key.
