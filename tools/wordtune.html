Wordtune Review 2026

Rewrite sentences so they sound clear, natural, and confident — with tone switching, grammar fixes, and AI summaries where you already write.

Overview

Wordtune rewrites your sentences so they sound clear, natural, and confident — switching tone from casual to formal, fixing grammar and spelling, and shortening or expanding text as you write. It works where you already write (browser, Word, Google Docs, mobile) and can also summarize long documents, articles, and even YouTube videos, so non-native English speakers and busy professionals alike stop second-guessing every email. IMPORTANT: AI21 Labs discontinued active Wordtune development in April 2025 to focus on enterprise AI — the product is still for sale and works, but it is in maintenance mode, not active development.

Official site: https://www.wordtune.com/

Key Features

The capabilities that matter most for US teams:

  • Best-in-class sentence rewriting: contextual suggestions that match your style, tone, and subject matter, not just grammar fixes
  • Genuine free plan — 10 rewrites a day, 3 AI summaries a month, unlimited spelling and grammar checks, no credit card required
  • Works everywhere via a top-rated Chrome extension (4.7/5) plus Word, Google Docs, iOS, and Android; 10M+ users and 782M rewrite suggestions chosen
  • Strong extras for a rewriter: AI summaries of documents/webpages/YouTube, Smart Translate into English from 10 languages, and fact-checked AI generations (checks 5+ sources)

❌ Cons

  • Development discontinued by its maker — AI21 Labs stopped active Wordtune development in April 2025 to focus on enterprise AI, so new features are unlikely
  • It is an editor, not a drafter — it polishes existing sentences but does not write long-form content from scratch like Jasper or ChatGPT
  • Free plan caps out fast (10 rewrites/day); the 3-day trial on paid plans demands payment details upfront, and Wordtune’s own FAQ states no refunds — cancel before the trial ends or you are charged
  • English-only output; the iOS app sells its own subscription at $24.99/month or $119.99/year — roughly 2.5× the web price — so mobile buyers can easily overpay
