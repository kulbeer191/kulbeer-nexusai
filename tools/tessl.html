Overview

As teams adopt several AI coding agents (Cursor, Claude Code, Codex, Antigravity), each agent accumulates its own skills, prompts and context — within two weeks an org can go from zero skills to hundreds of untrusted duplicates. Tessl is the control plane for that agent context: one searchable registry of skills and plugins, reusable agentic workflows ('loops') for code review, testing and incident work, and evals plus cost benchmarking so teams can pick the best model-and-agent combination for each task instead of guessing.

Official site: https://tessl.io

Key Features

The capabilities that matter most for US teams:

  • Searchable registry of agent skills and plugins across the org
  • Reusable agentic workflows ('loops') for review, testing and incidents
  • Evals and agent cost benchmarking in turns and tokens
  • Install/publish policies, mandatory org skills, audit logs
  • Works with Claude Code, Cursor, Codex, Antigravity and any agent

✅ Pros

  • One searchable registry across the org and private workspaces — find an existing skill before writing a duplicate
  • Agent- and model-agnostic — works with Claude Code, Cursor, Codex, Antigravity and 'any agent you run'; bring your own model
  • Built-in evals and agent cost benchmarking — benchmark model/harness combos against your own tasks and see real cost in turns and tokens, not just the rate card
  • Enterprise governance — install/publish policies, mandatory org skills, audit logs, SAML SSO, BYO LLM, single-tenant/self-hosted
  • Real free plan — $0 a month with 1,000 credits, no credit card needed

❌ Cons

  • Team plan jumps straight to $100 a month — steep for solo developers and small teams once free credits run out
  • Exact credit cost per action is NOT published on the pricing page ('exact credit costs are in the pricing reference') — spend is hard to predict, and frontier models burn credits faster
  • Credits expire monthly on Free and Team — unused credits do not roll over
  • Young, still-evolving platform — it has pivoted from spec-first development to a skills registry and 'software factory', so the feature set is still settling
  • Key enterprise features (BYO LLM, self-hosted, SAML SSO) are locked behind custom Enterprise pricing

Frequently asked questions

Is Tessl free?

Yes. The Free plan costs $0 a month with 1,000 credits, Tessl agent access and unlimited publish/install of plugins and skills, no credit card needed. The paid Team plan is $100 a month.

What is Tessl best for?

US engineering orgs standardising AI coding-agent use — a platform or DevEx team that wants one registry of approved skills, spending limits and install policies, or a team running several agents at once (Cursor + Claude Code + Codex) that wants consistent context, evals and cost control across all of them.

What should I watch out for with Tessl?

The Team plan jumps straight to $100 a month, and the exact credit cost per action is not published on the pricing page, so spend is hard to predict — frontier models burn credits faster. Credits expire monthly and do not roll over.

Verdict

Best for: US engineering orgs standardising AI coding-agent use — with the warning that Team jumps straight to $100 a month and exact credit costs per action are not published.
