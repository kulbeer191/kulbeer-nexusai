Overview

US support and CX teams need AI agents that actually resolve customer issues across chat AND phone — creating Zendesk tickets, touching Salesforce records, cancelling a Shopify order mid-conversation — not a black-box chatbot they cannot control. Voiceflow is a collaborative visual platform for designing, testing and deploying chat and voice agents: agentic playbooks blended with deterministic workflows, global instructions and guardrails, any major LLM (or your own model), native helpdesk/telephony integrations, and production tooling (environments, staging, evaluations, observability) — built by conversation-design teams, for teams that want to own their agent.

Official site: https://www.voiceflow.com/

Key Features

The capabilities that matter most for US teams:

  • Agentic playbooks blended with deterministic workflows and guardrails
  • Chat AND voice agents with live-agent handoff to major helpdesks
  • Any major LLM or your own model, with LLM fallback
  • Environments with staging, LLM evaluations, transcript/call analytics
  • Native Zendesk, Salesforce, HubSpot, Shopify, Twilio integrations

✅ Pros

  • Built for production CX, not demos — agentic playbooks + deterministic workflows + guardrails, environments with staging, LLM-powered evaluations, transcript/call analytics, and a claimed 99.95% agent uptime with capacity for 300,000 messages/minute
  • Real model choice with no lock-in — GPT 5.1 Codex, Claude Sonnet 4.5/Opus 4.1, Gemini 3 Pro, Llama 3, Grok Code and more, or bring your own model, with LLM fallback when a provider goes down
  • Deep native integrations — Zendesk, Salesforce, HubSpot, Shopify, Airtable, Google Sheets, Gmail, Twilio, plus REST APIs, custom functions and MCP servers; live-agent handoff to Zendesk, Genesys, UJET, Kustomer or Dixa
  • Strong compliance posture — SOC 2 Type II, ISO/IEC 27001:2022, GDPR, HIPAA-compliant deployments; customer data is not used to train models and model providers run with zero data retention

❌ Cons

  • No published prices on the public pricing page — voiceflow.com/pricing shows two cards ('For Agencies & Partners', 'For Businesses') and a free-trial mention, zero dollar figures. Voiceflow's own fact page says it does not publish list prices; buyers must ask sales
  • The credit meter can stop your agent mid-cycle — every message, call and model invocation consumes credits, and reports say agents stop responding when credits run out; credits do not roll over
  • Three-part billing makes the real cost hard to predict — base subscription + $50/month per extra editor seat + usage credits on top; a 5-editor team at 50,000 messages/month is roughly $450-$500/month, not the $60/$150 tier names

Frequently asked questions

Is Voiceflow free?

Yes. The Starter plan costs $0 with 100 credits a month, 2 agents, 50 knowledge sources and 1 editor — a genuine free tier for prototyping, plus a free trial with no credit card required. Paid plans start at Pro, $60 a month (corroborated — see pricing note).

What is Voiceflow best for?

US support and CX teams with a builder on staff who want to own a chat + voice agent across every channel — a support team resolving tickets across web chat and phone, an agency building white-labeled agents for multiple clients, or an enterprise CX team running scripted + agentic playbooks with guardrails and staging.

What should I watch out for with Voiceflow?

No prices are published on the public pricing page — buyers must ask sales, and the figures on this page are corroborated, not read off the vendor's site. The credit meter can stop your agent mid-cycle (credits do not roll over), and three-part billing (base + $50 per extra editor seat + usage credits) makes the real cost hard to predict.

Verdict

Best for: US support and CX teams with a builder on staff who want to own a chat + voice agent across every channel — with the warning that no prices are published on the public pricing page, and the credit meter can stop your agent mid-cycle.
