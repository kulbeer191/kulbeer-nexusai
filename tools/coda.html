Coda (Superhuman Docs) Review 2026

Overview

US ops, product and startup teams run their work across docs, spreadsheets, wikis and project trackers that never talk to each other — the plan lives in one tool, the data in another, and the status update in a third. Coda (Superhuman Docs) is a doc that behaves like an app: prose, relational tables, buttons, formulas and automations in one canvas, so a team can build a tracker, a wiki, a roadmap or an entire operating system without leaving the page — and Docs AI now builds those pages, answers questions across the workspace and automates the busywork. NOTE: On July 8, 2026, Coda became Superhuman Docs. Grammarly acquired Coda in Dec 2024, renamed the parent company to Superhuman on Oct 29, 2025, and rebranded the doc product itself the following July. The product is the same — all docs, workflows, tables, Packs, automations and formulas carry over, Maker Billing stays — but the name, URL and visual design changed, and the new home is docs.superhuman.com.

Official site: https://coda.io/

Key Features

The capabilities that matter most for US teams:

  • Docs that work like apps — relational tables, buttons, formulas, automations, 600+ Packs
  • Maker-only billing — only Doc Makers pay; editors and viewers are free
  • Docs AI (Beta) builds trackers and pages and answers questions across the workspace
  • Annual Pro includes Grammarly; Business adds Superhuman Go and Mail
  • Connected tables, views and forms; unlimited doc size on Pro

✅ Pros

  • Docs that work like apps — relational tables, buttons, formulas, automations and 600+ Packs (integrations) turn a page into a tracker, dashboard or workflow; no other doc tool in the directory goes this far
  • Maker-only billing — only Doc Makers (people who create or structure docs) pay; editors and viewers are free on every plan, so a 20-person team with 3 builders pays for 3, not 20
  • Real free plan — $0 with collaborative docs, Docs AI trial, MCP trial, connected tables/views/forms, formulas and automations
  • Now bundled with the Superhuman suite — annual Pro includes Grammarly, Business adds Superhuman Go and Mail, so teams already buying those get them in the same bill; Docs AI (Beta) builds trackers and pages and answers questions across the workspace

❌ Cons

  • The rename is genuinely confusing — coda.io/pricing redirects to superhuman.com/plans/docs, the page is titled 'Superhuman Docs', and the suite bundles Grammarly/Go/Mail in ways that make it hard to tell what you are actually buying
  • List prices rose at the rename — old widely-cited figures were Pro $10 / Team $30 per Doc Maker; the current published figures are $12 / $33 billed annually. The vendor's launch post says existing customers' pricing 'isn't changing', but new buyers pay the higher list prices — confirm at checkout
  • Docs AI is still in Beta — limited trial on Free, and the AI views/pages features are Beta; the AI story is newer and less proven than Notion AI's
  • 'Doc Maker' is a fuzzy billing unit — anyone who creates or structures docs counts, so a team that thought it had 3 makers can discover it has 8; and Grammarly/Superhuman ownership raised data questions in the Dec 2024 acquisition thread

Frequently asked questions

Is Coda free?

Yes. The Free plan costs $0 with collaborative docs, connected tables, views and forms, formulas and automations — Docs AI on Free is a limited trial only. Paid plans start at Pro, $12 a Doc Maker a month billed annually. Note the product is now Superhuman Docs (renamed July 8, 2026) — users still search for 'Coda'.

What is Coda best for?

US teams that want docs that behave like apps with maker-only billing — startups and ops teams building their operating system in one doc, product teams running launch trackers and specs where the doc IS the workflow, and small agencies managing client workspaces with only the agency's makers billed.

What should I watch out for with Coda?

The rename is genuinely confusing — coda.io/pricing redirects to superhuman.com/plans/docs and the suite bundles make it hard to tell what you are buying. List prices rose to $12/$33 at the rename, and 'Doc Maker' is a fuzzier billing unit than it looks — anyone who creates or structures docs counts.

Verdict

Best for: US teams that want docs that behave like apps with maker-only billing — with the warning that the product is now Superhuman Docs, list prices rose to $12/$33 at the rename, and 'Doc Maker' is a fuzzier billing unit than it looks.
