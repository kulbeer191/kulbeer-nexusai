Google Flow — Pros

  • Native audio in every clip — dialogue, ambient sound, and SFX generated together with the picture (Veo 3.1; Gemini Omni Flash also available in Flow)
  • Ingredients to Video keeps characters, objects, and style consistent across clips; Frames to Video bridges a start and end frame; Extend chains clips into minute-plus sequences
  • In-clip editing (Insert objects, Remove objects) plus Scenebuilder timeline assembly — no separate editor needed for rough cuts

Google Flow — Cons

  • Credit-based and expensive at the top — Pro's 1,000 monthly credits buy roughly 10 top-quality videos; real production work needs Ultra
  • Clips are short by default (4, 6, or 8 seconds); anything longer must be chained and extended by hand
  • No custom or brand voice selection yet, and some editing features reach Flow later than the API

Runway — Pros

  • Flagship Gen-4.5 model with strong motion consistency, realistic physics, and character stability across clips
  • Full creative suite in one place: text/image-to-video, video editing (Aleph), performance transfer (Act-Two), plus image and audio generation
  • Also hosts third-party models (Veo, Kling, Seedance) under one credit system

Runway — Cons

  • Credit-based pricing makes costs hard to predict — heavy users burn through monthly credits fast
  • Clips are short (2–10 seconds for Gen-4.5), so longer stories need many generations and stitching
  • Free-plan output is watermarked and limited to a one-time 125-credit allotment that never renews

Frequently asked questions

Which is cheaper, Google Flow or Runway?

Both have a free $0 plan. Google Flow's entry paid plan is Plus at $4.99 a month (200 credits); Runway's is Standard at $15 a month (625 credits). Flow is cheaper to start, but its credits buy fewer top-quality videos — Pro's 1,000 credits cover roughly 10 top-quality videos, while Runway's credits stretch further across its model range.

Which should I choose, Google Flow or Runway?

Pick Google Flow if you want cinematic clips with dialogue, ambient sound and effects baked in, and characters that stay consistent across shots. Pick Runway if you want a full creative suite — video editing (Aleph), performance transfer (Act-Two), image and audio generation, plus third-party models under one credit system.

What should I watch out for?

Both are credit-based, so costs are hard to predict and heavy use burns credits fast. Flow's clips are short (4–8 seconds) and need chaining for longer sequences; Runway's free plan is a one-time 125-credit allotment that never renews, and its output is watermarked.

Verdict

Pick Google Flow if native audio and character consistency matter most — cinematic shorts with dialogue and SFX baked in. Pick Runway if you want the full creative suite with editing, performance transfer and third-party models under one credit system.
